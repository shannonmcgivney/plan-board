# Plan board: messy plan in, shared board out

**Why (teach it this way):** a plan in a chat or a voice memo is invisible to the team and has no owners. A board shows the whole plan at a glance, everyone can move things around live, and Claude does the boring part (sorting, ordering, dating, drafting action items). People still decide: Claude drafts, people check.

## Facts (checked 2026-11-13)
- Claude Whiteboards launched in **beta on 2026-11-10**, on **all paid plans**, on web, desktop and mobile.
- **Team** plans: on by default. **Enterprise**: off by default; an admin turns it on in Organization settings > Artifacts.
- From Anthropic's launch post: talk through a messy plan and Claude makes "a timeline with owners and dates"; in a retro Claude groups notes by theme and drafts action items per person; drag a box round part of the board to ask about just that part; turn a board into a doc or deck; bring it into Lucid, Miro or Vani by Zoho.
- Seen in social posts but not in the launch post: dictation in 25 languages, PNG export, "follow a teammate", a Whiteboard tile in the Artifacts tab. Treat these as unconfirmed until you see them in the app.
- It's a beta. Features and plans may change, so check claude.com before teaching from these facts.

## Three modes

### 1. Plan (brain dump → timeline)
1. Take the input as is: pasted text, a voice-memo transcript, a meeting transcript, a Slack thread. Don't make the user clean it up first.
2. Pull out: the goal, the steps, who owns each, dates or durations, dependencies, and open questions.
3. **Never invent owners or dates.** Unknown owner → "Owner?"; unknown date → leave it blank and list it under Open questions. Turn relative dates ("next Friday") into real dates.
4. Lay it out left to right as a timeline (start a second row after 6 steps): one block per step with owner and date, arrows only for real dependencies, a section for Open questions. Put a title and a one-sentence takeaway on top.

### 2. Retro (notes → themes → action items)
1. Columns: **Went well · Slowed us down · Try next** (or the team's own).
2. Group the notes by theme inside each column. Keep people's words.
3. Draft **one action item per person** (owner initials, a short task that starts with a verb, a due date if one was given) in a section under the columns, marked as Claude's draft.
4. Good sources: review notes on a project, a class's end-of-week feedback, a launch week's results.

### 3. Hand-off (board → doc or deck)
When the thinking is done, offer to turn the board into a doc (a project plan) or a slide deck, and keep a link back to the board in it. Exports to Lucid, Miro or Vani happen in the Whiteboard app itself.

## How to build it
- **In Claude (web, desktop, mobile):** ask for it in the chat: "Start a whiteboard and lay out this plan as a timeline with owners and dates: …". Then refine on the board, or drag a box round part of it and ask about that part.
- **If your Claude can create artifacts from types** (it can list a "Whiteboard" type): create the board from that type and follow the type's own instructions for writing to it.
- Use shapes, text and arrows only. A plan or a retro has nothing to draw, so no pictures.
- Reply in 2-3 sentences: what's on the board, what was assumed, what's still open. Then give the link.
- Boards are private until their owner shares them. Don't share, send or post one without the user's go.

## Teaching notes (for instructors)
- Have learners try it on a real plan: "Start a whiteboard. Here's my messy plan for [job search / side business / event]: …"
- Then: drag a box round two steps and ask "what's missing between these?"
- Point out that it's a beta, and that people at Enterprise companies may need an admin to switch it on.
- It needs a paid plan. On the free plan the same thinking works as a table in a chat: steps, owner, date, open questions.
